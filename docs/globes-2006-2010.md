## Historical scope and dates

Animated Feature was first awarded at the 2007 ceremony, with three nominees. It retained three in 2008 and 2009 and expanded to five in 2010. The 2006 page therefore omits the category; the later historical pages preserve their real nominee counts. Official category announcements:

- https://goldenglobes.com/articles/new-award-category-best-animated-feature-film-commences-64th-annual-golden-globes/
- https://goldenglobes.com/articles/best-animated-feature-film-category-expands-five-contenders-67th-annual-golden-globe-awards/

The 2006 Director and Actor — Musical or Comedy categories each had six nominees. The 2008 Drama category had seven. These larger lists are deliberate and match the contemporary official announcements.

The traditional 2008 ceremony was cancelled during the writers' strike. Awards were still given, with winners announced at an HFPA press conference on 13 January. This edition uses `announcement_date` and the label “Winners announcement”, rather than asserting a ceremony took place. The source event includes an explanation. The official 7 January announcement and 11 January production statement establish this distinction:

- https://goldenglobes.com/articles/recipients-65th-annual-golden-globe-awards-be-announced-beverly-hilton-press-conference/
- https://goldenglobes.com/articles/golden-globe-awards-press-conference-set-sunday-be-produced-hollywood-foreign-press/

Each of the other four source editions includes `ceremony_date` and an official date-source URL.

## Source evidence and repairs

`imports/globes-2006-2010-source.json` is normalized from the official `/wp-json/awdb/v1/winners-and-nominees/?year=YEAR` API. It retains nomination IDs, original category names and individual film URLs. `scripts/collect-globes-2006-2010.py` reproduces the collection with explicit historical category-size checks.

The contemporary [63rd nominations announcement](https://goldenglobes.com/articles/63rd-golden-globe-awards-nominations/) resolves three database defects:

- *Munich* has separate API nomination rows for co-writers Tony Kushner and Eric Roth. They are combined into one film nomination with both writers; both source IDs (69980 and 73121) are preserved in `source_ids`.
- *Good Night, and Good Luck* restores co-nominee Grant Heslov alongside George Clooney.
- *Crash* restores co-nominee Bobby Moresco alongside Paul Haggis.

The source also corrects the duplicated API stage name “Mo’Nique Mo’nique” to “Mo'Nique”. The contemporary BET/Paramount [winner announcement](https://ir.paramount.com/static-files/0050ba1c-0090-4d55-b1d5-3becd2b038c9) confirms the single stage name and award. Original labels, source IDs and correction provenance are retained. These repairs neither invent a nomination nor omit a nominated person.

Title-only identity aliases and reviewed director credits are recorded separately in `imports/globes-2006-2010-identity-overrides.json`; public catalogue identities and private progress are left to the shared batch generator. The foreign-language compound titles preserve the original official source text.

Additional scoped provider-credit checks cover *Babel* (Alejandro González Iñárritu / Alejandro G. Iñárritu / Alejandro Iñárritu) and *Dreamgirls* (Beyoncé Knowles / Beyoncé). The official Globes director biography establishes Iñárritu's full name, and its [Dreamgirls retrospective](https://goldenglobes.com/articles/2007-musical-or-comedy-dreamgirls/) explicitly states that Beyoncé was billed as Beyoncé Knowles in the film. These aliases preserve the original source nomination names. *Nine* is fixed to IMDb tt0875034, Rob Marshall and its three nominated performers, distinguishing the musical from unrelated films and the animated *9*.
