# BAFTA film awards 2011–2015

`imports/bafta-2011-2015-source.json` contains the established major film categories as they actually existed in each ceremony year. Documentary is absent in 2011 and has three nominees when it returns in 2012. The other included categories are Best Film, Director, Outstanding British Film, four acting categories, Original and Adapted Screenplay, Film Not in the English Language and Animated Film. No shorts, craft, debut, Rising Star or honorary categories are added.

The batch contains 59 categories, 285 nomination records and 134 distinct normalized film titles. Animated Film has three nominees in all five years. Every category has exactly one winning nomination. Multiple nominated performances for one film remain separate, including Annette Bening and Julianne Moore for The Kids Are All Right, Octavia Spencer and Jessica Chastain for The Help, and Mark Ruffalo and Steve Carell for Foxcatcher.

Each edition uses BAFTA's official results archive as its source and records a separate `contemporary_source` for the original official nomination/winner document. All 285 film titles and nominated-person credits were checked within their corresponding category against these contemporary documents, with punctuation, case, accents and nominee order normalized. Ceremony dates have individual official `date_source` evidence. The archive's older “Film” category label maps to the established “Best Film” label.

## Archive corrections

The comparison found two errors in the current archive; both are corrected and recorded in the source evidence:

- **Incendies, 2012 Film Not in the English Language:** the archive misspells producer Kim McCraw as “Kim McGraw”. The [contemporary nomination PDF](https://static.bafta.org/files/nominees-1112-1282.pdf) supplies Kim McCraw.
- **Les Misérables, 2013 Outstanding British Film:** the archive truncates the nominee list after “Claud”. The [contemporary nomination PDF](https://static.bafta.org/files/film-1213-nominees-1647.pdf) supplies the complete list, including Claude-Michel Schönberg and Herbert Kretzmer.

No person-as-title corruptions remain. Source title variants such as Untouchable (The Intouchables), The Adventures of Tintin: The Secret of the Unicorn, and Birdman retain their official spellings for audited provider matching. The 2011 Girl with the Dragon Tattoo nominations refer to Niels Arden Oplev's Swedish film, identified by the director and Noomi Rapace credit, rather than David Fincher's later film.
