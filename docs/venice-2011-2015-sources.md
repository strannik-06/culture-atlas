# Venice 2011–2015: official source audit

This batch covers Venezia Competition and the competing feature films in Orizzonti. Jury prizes annotate selected films; short/medium-length works, special and out-of-competition screenings, events, retrospective programmes, parallel sections, honorary and collateral awards are excluded.

## Official archive evidence and reproducibility

La Biennale's official ASAC annual archive supplies the complete section membership, official jury prizes and festival dates:

- https://asac.labiennale.org/attivita/cinema/annali?anno=2011
- https://asac.labiennale.org/attivita/cinema/annali?anno=2012
- https://asac.labiennale.org/attivita/cinema/annali?anno=2013
- https://asac.labiennale.org/attivita/cinema/annali?anno=2014
- https://asac.labiennale.org/attivita/cinema/annali?anno=2015

Every selected film retains its official individual archive URL, original/distribution title aliases, director, production year and runtime. The normalized evidence is generated by `scripts/collect-venice-2011-2015.py`, using the public ASAC API requested by the archive's own web interface. The annual records use `callIndex: annali_cinema`, while film records use `callIndex: cinema`; both are fetched from `/api/ricerca-semplice`. The collector checks complete record counts and fails on missing film identities or directors.

The archive stores festival boundaries as UTC instants corresponding to local midnight in Venice. The collector converts those instants to Europe/Rome calendar dates. Explicit closing award-ceremony dates are included where the archive has a matching ceremony record (2011, 2013 and 2015); all five editions have exact festival date ranges.

## Historical scope and archive exceptions

**2011 Orizzonti:** the annual section combines 52 feature, medium-length and short works. The contemporary final programme's feature list contains 24 works, including Edgardo Cozarinsky's 63-minute *Nocturnos*. ASAC currently labels that film “Mediometraggio”, while the published contemporary selection places it among features, before the separate “Orizzonti mediometraggi e cortometraggi” heading. This batch follows the historical programme placement and includes it; the other medium-length works are excluded. The contemporary selection reproduction is available at [Stanze di Cinema, 27 July 2011](https://stanzedicinema.com/2011/07/27/venezia-2011-tutti-i-film-di-orizzonti/) and [FilmTV's contemporary announcement](https://www.filmtv.it/post/5083/festival-di-venezia-2011-i-film-della-sezione-orizzonti/). [Screen's announcement](https://www.screendaily.com/venice/venice-horizons-selections-include-jonathan-demme-ross-mcelwee-docs/5030283.article) explicitly states 24 features. Official individual record: https://asac.labiennale.org/attivita/cinema/225376 .

*Louyre — This Our Still Life* belongs to the separate medium/short subsection in that historical announcement and remains excluded; its ASAC duration is also inconsistent with the announcement. The retrospective “Orizzonti 1960–1978” and “Orizzonti - Eventi” are separate sections and excluded.

**2013 Orizzonti:** the annual archive combines 31 feature and short entries. *Death for a Unicorn* is incorrectly typed “Lungometraggio” but has its correct 15-minute runtime. It is excluded, leaving 17 features. The contemporaneous programme labels the screening “Orizzonti Corti”; the runtime check prevents the archive classification error from introducing a short-film catalogue identity. Official record: https://asac.labiennale.org/attivita/cinema/235143 .

**2014 Orizzonti:** *Words with Gods* is explicitly in “Orizzonti - Fuori Concorso” and excluded, leaving the 17 competing features.

## Prizes as actually awarded

The batch does not backfill modern categories into earlier editions. The main competition's Grand Jury Prize appears from 2013; earlier Special Jury Prizes retain their historical designation. The 2011 Osella for Cinematography and 2012 Best Technical Contribution for Cinematography are included as official main-jury prizes. Orizzonti has only its actual prize categories: two in 2012, the innovative-content prize in 2013, and one combined acting prize in 2014–2015. No separate actor/actress or screenplay prizes are invented for those editions.

Shared recipients are retained: Sometani and Nikaidô for *Himizu* (2011); Filippou and Lanthimos for *Alpeis* (2011); Phoenix and Hoffman for *The Master* (2012); Coogan and Pope for *Philomena* (2013); Mostafavi and Bani-Etemad for *Ghesse-ha* (2014).

The 2012 ASAC annual record attaches the Marcello Mastroianni Award to Fabrizio Falco but leaves its work reference empty. The [Italian Ministry of Culture's official announcement](https://cinema.cultura.gov.it/notizie/mostra-del-cinema-di-venezia-vince-pieta-premi-ai-film-di-interesse-culturale-a-falco-il-mastroianni-per-le-interpretazioni-di-e-stato-il-figlio-e-bella-addormentata-a-cipri-per-la-fotografia/) explicitly identifies both *È stato il figlio* and *Bella addormentata*. Both films receive the annotation, with this supplementary source stored on the prize record.

Lion of the Future is included only for selected competing features: *Court* (2014) and *The Childhood of a Leader* (2015). The 2011–2013 debut winners were in parallel sections and remain outside the batch.

No private ratings or live database rows are modified by collection.
