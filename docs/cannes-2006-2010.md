Total: 206 selections and 61 prize records. Sources are `https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/retrospective/{year}/selection/` and the corresponding `/awards/` for each year 2006–2010. `scripts/collect-cannes-2006-2010.mjs` reproduces normalization from downloaded official retrospective HTML. All selection identities and included prize references pass local structural validation.

The established scope includes main and Un Certain Regard jury prizes and special mentions, plus Caméra d’or for films already in these selections. Hunger (2008) and Samson and Delilah (2009) therefore receive Caméra d’or annotations. Parallel sidebars, shorts, Cinéfondation, independent juries and CST technical prizes are excluded.

## Historical special awards

- Paranoid Park receives the work-specific 60th Anniversary Prize (2007), confirmed by its [official film page](https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/f/paranoid-park/) and [award announcement](https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/2007/60th-anniversary-award-gus-van-sant-for-paranoid-park/).
- The 2008 special prizes for Catherine Deneuve and Clint Eastwood are excluded as career awards. The [contemporary official French announcement](https://www.festival-cannes.com/2008/prix-du-61eme-festival-de-cannes-catherine-deneuve-et-clint-eastwood/) explicitly awards them for their entire careers. They are not converted into film prizes for A Christmas Tale or Changeling.
- The 2009 lifetime achievement award for Alain Resnais is likewise excluded, even though the retrospective places it alongside Wild Grass. The source label explicitly identifies lifetime achievement and exceptional contribution to cinema.
- The 2006 ensemble actress award for Volver retains all six names; the actor award for Days of Glory retains all five names. The 2010 shared actress award for The Lips retains all three recipients.

## Che: the complete festival film

The 2008 Che selection is the complete two-part feature, not only Che: Part One or Che: Part Two. Its [official Cannes page](https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/f/che/) identifies Steven Soderbergh, production year 2008, both part synopses, and 268 minutes. The [official English press kit](https://cdn-media.festival-cannes.com/film_film/0001/89/c31dc22f36be410e543e5955d0a2ba97c407b8e9.pdf) specifies Part One at 137 minutes and Part Two at 131 minutes. The [contemporary competition article](https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/2008/competition-che-by-steven-soderbergh/), dated 21 May 2008, confirms the combined four-hour-and-28-minute presentation.

The [Criterion director-approved edition](https://www.criterion.com/films/20987-che) also identifies the complete two-part work as Che (2008), with a later presentation duration of 271 minutes. These presentation differences do not justify replacing the whole festival selection with one part. TMDB movie 8881 / IMDb tt0892255 is Part One; IMDb tt0374569 is Part Two. Neither individual-part identifier is an identity for the complete festival work. Research did not establish a reliable combined TMDB movie or TV record; the whole-work identity must remain explicit during catalogue resolution.

The reviewed manual identity is `festival:cannes:2008:che`, with no individual-part provider links. It uses an original synopsis based on both official part summaries, the 21 May 2008 festival premiere date, 268-minute festival runtime and combined-work Criterion cast/artwork. The Cannes retrospective's cover was visually inspected and rejected for this identity: its small print explicitly identifies Part One. The Criterion cover is taken from the publisher's complete-film page.

Paris, je t’aime has another documented archival issue: Cannes renders Oliver Schmitz as “Oliver”. The reviewed name groups retain the complete collaborator list and allow Emmanuel Benbihy's transition-sequence credit, verified with the film's official press kit and the San Sebastian festival listing. Neither this exception nor the Che identity changes the underlying official selection evidence.

The event-specific identity override file contains 25 reviewed entries, including Che and Paris, je t’aime. All 24 Cannes records unresolved by the first provider pass have evidence-backed title/name variants or independently researched IMDb pins. Other important credit repairs include Eric Khoo for My Magic (the archive says only “Eric”), both directors of Ten Canoes (the producer credits Peter Djigirr as co-director), the complete five-director group for Tales from the Golden Age, and removal of the cinematographer-association suffix “ABC” from César Charlone's name in The Pope's Toilet. None of these exceptions disables full director-credit verification or replaces an ambiguous result with a title-only guess.
